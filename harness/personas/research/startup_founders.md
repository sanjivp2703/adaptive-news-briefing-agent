# Startup founders / venture capital — news events, 2026-08-05 to 2026-09-05

Research conducted 2026-09-06. Window covered: roughly 2026-08-05 through 2026-09-05, with two items whose
announcement fell a day or two before the window but whose *completion* or continued coverage sits inside it
(noted individually). Every claim below comes from a page fetched in this session; search-result snippets were
treated as unreliable and discarded wherever the fetched page disagreed with them.

13 events: 3 clearly major, 6 borderline, 4 clearly minor.

**Every `headline` is written to be true standing completely alone**, with no support from the fields beneath
it. Where a claim is attributed, unconfirmed, or contested, the attribution or qualifier is inside the headline
text itself rather than left to `status` or `confidence_note`. Contested figures are kept out of headlines
entirely. Do not rewrite a headline into a more assertive form for brevity — the hedges are load-bearing.

**Every event carries a `status` field.** Most read `occurred`. One — the Stripe/OpenRouter deal — reads
**`reported, not confirmed`** and additionally carries a warning banner. Nothing in this file should be treated
as a completed event unless its `status` says so. A rumoured or in-talks transaction is a real *news event*
(the report was published) but not a real *occurrence*, and a test suite that conflates the two will reward a
grader for asserting things that never happened.

One candidate was deliberately **excluded** on exactly these grounds: reporting on 2026-08-24 and 2026-08-26
that **Nvidia was in advanced talks to acquire Hugging Face for ~$12.9B**. It was widely covered (The
Information, Business Insider, TechCrunch) and would otherwise qualify as major, but Business Insider
explicitly noted the talks had not produced a signed agreement and could still fall apart. It is recorded here
as an exclusion rather than as an event.

# Knowledge decay in this domain

**Short answer: the half-life is roughly 2–6 weeks for individual events, and 1–2 quarters for market-conditions reads. But the decay is not uniform — different classes of fact expire on very different clocks, and a single decay parameter will misfit unless it accounts for that.**

The concrete argument, by fact type:

**1. Funding-round specifics decay fastest — 2 to 4 weeks of conversational currency.** A round is "news" for about the length of one news cycle and "current" for about a month. After that it stops being something people mention and becomes background: nobody in October says "did you see Lovable raised?" — they say "Lovable, which is at $13B." Critically, the *valuation attached to a company* is the fastest-rotting single fact in this domain. Instinct went from roughly $50–100M to $2.5B in weeks; Databricks moved from $134B (February) to $188B (July) to $190B (August) inside seven months. Any statement of the form "Company X is worth $Y" has an expected accuracy half-life measured in months, not years, for the top ~100 AI companies. Round *stage* labels (Series B vs C) go stale even faster in the sense that they get re-labeled or skipped entirely.

**2. Reported-but-unconfirmed deals decay in days — and they are the single biggest correctness hazard here.** Three of the largest stories in this window — Stripe/OpenRouter, Nvidia/Hugging Face, Nvidia/Poolside — were sourced to Bloomberg, The Information and Newcomer respectively, and at least one was explicitly described as possibly falling apart. These are the highest-decay facts in the entire domain: within 2–4 weeks each will have either closed at a possibly different price, been repriced, or died. The specific failure mode is that the confident framing survives while the "in talks" qualifier gets dropped, so a model repeating "Nvidia bought Hugging Face for $13B" two months later is wrong in a way that is *worse* than saying nothing. This is why Nvidia/Hugging Face is excluded from this file entirely and Stripe/OpenRouter is flagged rather than stated. For decay purposes, treat "reported deal" and "closed deal" as two different fact types with half-lives an order of magnitude apart.

**3. Completed acquisitions and IPO events barely decay at all — years.** SpaceX/Cursor at $60B, Bending Spoons/Airtable, the Unitree listing: these are terminal events. They become permanent reference points and get cited for years as comps and cautionary tales. This is the slowest-decaying category and it is why a flat decay rate underfits.

**4. Market-conditions reads decay on a 1–2 quarter cycle.** Statements like "August global venture funding was $42B, down 25% from July but up 122% year over year," or "seed-to-Series A graduation has collapsed," or "AI is absorbing most of the dollars" stay directionally valid for about two quarters and then need re-verification. The *direction* often persists longer than the *number*. The trap is that these reads feel timeless — "it's hard to raise a Series A" sounds like a permanent truth — but the underlying figures move enough that quoting a specific percentage from three quarters ago is usually wrong.

**5. People and roles decay on a 6–18 month clock.** Jacob Bank running Chrome product, Elias Torres as Klaviyo CPO, a founder's age and company. These are moderately durable but turn over on hiring cycles.

**6. Essays and framings decay slowest of all — years to permanent.** Nothing in this window's essay category verified cleanly enough to include, but structurally, a widely-read founder essay stays quotable for years; it becomes vocabulary rather than news.

**Practical calibration:** if forced to a single number, use a half-life of about **3 weeks** for "is this still live news," because the modal event in this domain is a funding round or a reported deal. But the correct model is bimodal: reported/pending items should decay in **days**, closed transactions and IPOs should be treated as **near-permanent**, and market statistics should carry an explicit **~2 quarter** expiry with the direction outliving the number. The domain's overall velocity in this window was unusually high — a $60B acquisition closing, a $13B acquisition in talks, a $7B acquisition reported, and a 460% IPO pop all inside four weeks — so anything calibrated on a calmer period will under-decay.
