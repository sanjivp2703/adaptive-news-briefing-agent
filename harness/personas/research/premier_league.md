# Knowledge decay in this domain

Premier League knowledge decays **fast, but at three clearly different rates depending on the category of fact** — this domain is unusual in that the decay curve is not uniform.

**Results decay in roughly 7 days.** The competition runs on a weekly cycle, and each matchweek overwrites the last as the conversational default. A result is live from full-time until roughly the following Wednesday's preview cycle; after the next round of fixtures it survives only as a table position, not as an event. "Hull beat Manchester United" was headline news for about four days and then became a line in the standings. The exceptions are results that feed an ongoing narrative (a manager under pressure, a record, a title-race swing) — those get re-cited for weeks because pundits keep reaching for them. International breaks are a hard discontinuity: a two-week gap kills the conversational life of the preceding matchweek almost entirely, and there is one immediately after the window covered here.

**Transfer knowledge decays in two stages.** During an open window, rumour and "linked with" information is worthless within 24-48 hours — it is superseded constantly, and much of it never becomes fact. But once the window *shuts*, the completed-deals set freezes and becomes durable reference knowledge: who a club signed in summer 2026 stays correct and relevant for the whole season and is still checkable years later. So the interesting property is that transfer knowledge is highly perishable right up to 23:00 on 1 September and then becomes nearly non-perishable. Deadline-day *drama* (the Fofana jets, the collapsed Tottenham bid) decays much faster than the deal itself — the fee and the destination survive; the narrative around them fades within a fortnight.

**Injury knowledge decays over the stated timeline, and continuously.** "Rodon is out for 8-10 weeks" is true on the day it's said and progressively less true every week after: at four weeks it is stale but not wrong, at ten weeks it is actively misleading. Injury facts are the fastest-degrading *correctness* claims in the domain because the underlying state changes without any announcement — a player quietly returns and no one publishes "player is now fit". Fitness/availability questions therefore need a much shorter freshness horizon than transfer or result questions.

**Managerial and governance facts are the slow layer.** "Newcastle's head coach is Matthias Jaissle" stays true for months or years and is the kind of fact people carry as background state. Rule changes (the raised VAR intervention bar, the front-of-shirt gambling sponsorship ban) are season-scale — true and relevant for a full year. Squad numbers, academy appointments and youth loans are technically season-durable but nobody retains them, so their decay is about *attention*, not *truth*.

**Practical calibration:** a single decay-rate parameter should be tuned toward the fast end — a **half-life on the order of one to two weeks** for the domain's centre of mass, because match results dominate the volume of news and turn over weekly. But the parameter under-serves two tails: deadline-day completed transfers and managerial identity, which should be treated as roughly season-durable (months), and injury-availability claims, which should be treated as shorter than the headline rate (days once a stated return window approaches).
